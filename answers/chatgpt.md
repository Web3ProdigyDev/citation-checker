**Fertilizer application:**

- Apply the basal fertilizer at planting or within the first week after planting.
- Split nitrogen application, applying the remaining nitrogen approximately 4–5 weeks after planting.
- Incorporate fertilizer into the soil rather than leaving it exposed on the surface.
- Apply phosphorus and potassium basally, according to the recommended fertilizer formulation.

**Practical recommendation:** Use the appropriate regional recommendation as a starting point and adjust it using soil-test results, particularly for phosphorus and potassium.

## **F2-NG**

**How is fall armyworm on maize controlled in Nigeria, and which methods are officially recommended?**

Fall armyworm (*Spodoptera frugiperda*) is an important maize pest in Nigeria. Its larvae feed on maize leaves, growing points and developing cobs, potentially causing substantial yield losses.

Control in Nigeria involves a combination of cultural, mechanical, biological and chemical methods.

**1. Cultural control**

- Practise early planting to reduce exposure to heavy pest infestations.
- Practise intercropping, particularly with suitable legumes.
- Use push-pull technology, where suitable companion crops help repel the pest and attract it away from maize.
- Practise field sanitation and destroy infested crop residues.
- Maintain good crop nutrition and general field management.

Sources: [FAO, Fall Armyworm Management in Africa](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/story/Five-things-to-know-about-African-farmers-latest-foe---the-Fall-Armyworm/en?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [FAO, Integrated Management of Fall Armyworm](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1112643/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**2. Mechanical control**

- Regularly inspect maize plants for egg masses and young larvae.
- Handpick and destroy egg masses and caterpillars.
- Remove heavily infested plant parts where practical.
- Apply ash, sand or soil into the maize whorl as a traditional control option.

Source: [FAO, Five Things to Know About Fall Armyworm](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/story/Five-things-to-know-about-African-farmers-latest-foe---the-Fall-Armyworm/en?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**3. Biological control**

Biological control involves protecting or introducing organisms that naturally attack fall armyworm.

Examples include:

- Parasitoid wasps such as *Trichogramma* and *Telenomus*.
- Predators that feed on eggs and larvae.
- Entomopathogenic fungi and bacteria, including suitable *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) products.
- Botanical pesticides, including neem-based products.

Sources: [FAO, Fall Armyworm Management: Farmer Field School Experiences](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1396853/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [FAO, Integrated Management Guide](https://assets.accessagriculture.org/s3fs-public/FAO_FFS_guide_for_FAW_control.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**4. Chemical control**

Chemical insecticides may be necessary when infestation reaches economically damaging levels.

However:

- Use only registered and recommended insecticides.
- Apply products according to their approved label rates and instructions.
- Target young larvae because they are generally easier to control.
- Avoid unnecessary spraying and rotate insecticide modes of action to reduce resistance.
- Avoid indiscriminate spraying that destroys beneficial organisms.

Sources: [FAO Integrated Management Guide](https://assets.accessagriculture.org/s3fs-public/FAO_FFS_guide_for_FAW_control.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and [FAO Fall Armyworm IPM Steps](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/rne/FAW/Rollup_IPM_STEPS_En.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |

It is important to distinguish documented farmer practices from formally approved national recommendations. The NAERLS survey records practices used in the states; it is not a pesticide registration list.

**Conclusion:** Integrated pest management is the preferred approach. Cultural prevention, field monitoring, mechanical removal and biological control should be prioritized, with registered insecticides used when necessary. Specific chemical products should be checked against current Nigerian registration and extension guidance.

- Predatory insects.

- Regular field scouting.
- Correct identification of fall armyworm and its damage.

- Understanding the role of natural enemies in reducing pest populations.

## **H4-NG**

**What is Lassa fever, how is it transmitted, and how is it treated in Nigeria?**

Lassa fever is an acute viral illness caused by the Lassa virus, which belongs to the Arenaviridae family. It is a zoonotic disease, meaning it spreads from animals to humans.

The disease was first identified in 1969 in Lassa, Borno State, Nigeria. It is endemic in Nigeria and several other West African countries.

**Transmission**

The primary source of infection is the multimammate rat (*Mastomys natalensis*).

Humans can become infected through:

- Eating food contaminated with infected rats' urine or faeces.
- Touching contaminated household objects or surfaces.
- Direct contact with infected rodents.
- Contact with the blood, urine, faeces or other bodily fluids of an infected person.
- Exposure to contaminated medical equipment, particularly in healthcare settings with inadequate infection prevention measures.

Symptoms may include fever, weakness, headache, sore throat, vomiting, diarrhoea, muscle pain and abdominal pain. Severe cases can cause bleeding, facial swelling, hearing loss and organ failure.

Sources:

- [Nigeria Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), Lassa Fever](https://ncdc.gov.ng/diseases/info/Lassa?utm_source=chatgpt.com).
- [WHO Lassa Fever Fact Sheet, 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/Lassa-fever?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**Treatment in Nigeria**

Treatment should take place in designated isolation or treatment centres under the supervision of trained healthcare professionals.

Treatment involves:

1. **Antiviral medication:** Nigeria's NCDC guidelines identify intravenous ribavirin as the drug of choice, with better outcomes traditionally associated with early administration.
2. **Supportive care:** Fluid and electrolyte replacement, nutritional support and management of complications.
3. **Monitoring:** Regular assessment of vital signs, kidney and liver function, and other complications.
4. **Infection prevention:** Isolation of infected patients and strict protective measures for healthcare workers.

An important qualification is that WHO's December 2024 guidance notes that the effectiveness of ribavirin remains uncertain and that further clinical research is needed. It nevertheless emphasizes early intensive supportive care as essential.

There is currently no approved vaccine for Lassa fever.

## **H4-GL**

**What is dengue fever, how is it transmitted, and how is it treated?**

Dengue fever, sometimes called break-bone fever, is a mosquito-borne viral infection caused by the dengue virus (DENV).

It is common in tropical and subtropical regions, particularly in urban and semi-urban environments.

According to WHO, approximately half of the world's population is at risk of dengue, with an estimated 100–400 million infections occurring annually.

Source: [WHO Dengue Fact Sheet, 21 August 2025](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dengue-and-severe-dengue?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**Transmission**

Dengue is primarily transmitted through the bite of infected female *Aedes* mosquitoes, especially *Aedes aegypti*.

The transmission process works as follows:

1. A mosquito bites a person infected with dengue.
2. The virus enters and multiplies inside the mosquito.
3. The infected mosquito bites another person and transmits the virus.

Dengue does not ordinarily spread directly from one person to another through casual contact.

**Symptoms**

Common symptoms include:

- High fever.
- Severe headache.
- Pain behind the eyes.
- Muscle and joint pain.
- Nausea and vomiting.
- Skin rash.

Severe dengue can cause internal bleeding, plasma leakage, shock and organ damage. Warning signs include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums, rapid breathing and extreme weakness.

Source: [WHO Dengue Fact Sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/dengue-and-severe-dengue?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**Treatment**

There is currently no specific antiviral treatment for dengue. Management focuses on relieving symptoms and preventing complications.

WHO recommends:

1. Adequate fluid intake and rest.
2. Paracetamol to manage fever and pain.
3. Avoiding aspirin and ibuprofen because they can increase the risk of bleeding.
4. Close medical monitoring for warning signs of severe dengue.
5. Hospitalization and carefully managed fluid replacement for severe cases.

Early diagnosis and appropriate medical care significantly improve the chances of survival.

**How does health insurance work in Nigeria under the National Health Insurance Authority Act?**

Nigeria's National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) Act 2022 established the National Health Insurance Authority, replacing the National Health Insurance Scheme Act of 1999.

The Act was signed into law on 19 May 2022. Its primary objective is to expand health insurance coverage, improve access to healthcare and reduce direct out-of-pocket healthcare expenses.

Source: [NHIA Act 2022, Official NHIA Website](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/nhia-act/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**1. Mandatory health insurance**

Section 14 of the Act requires every person resident in Nigeria to obtain health insurance.

This includes:

- Employees and employers in the public and private sectors with five or more employees.
- Informal-sector workers.
- Other residents of Nigeria.

Individuals may also obtain private health insurance, subject to participation in applicable state-mandated health schemes.

Source: [NHIA Act 2022, Section 14](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NHIA-Act-2022-Gazetted-Copy.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**2. How the insurance system operates**

The system operates through different insurance arrangements:

- **Formal sector:** Employees and employers participate through employment-based health insurance arrangements.
- **Informal sector:** Self-employed individuals, traders and other informal workers can enrol through available state health insurance schemes and other approved arrangements.
- **Private insurance:** Individuals and organizations can purchase additional private health insurance coverage.
- **State health insurance schemes:** State Social Health Insurance Agencies manage insurance programmes for residents in their respective states.

Source: [NHIA State Social Health Insurance Schemes](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/service/home-insurance/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**3. Coverage for vulnerable populations**

The Act provides for vulnerable groups, including poor and disadvantaged Nigerians, to receive health insurance coverage without paying premiums.

Section 13(7) requires states and the Federal Capital Territory to provide coverage for eligible vulnerable persons through the Basic Health Care Provision Fund (BHCPF) and other funding sources.

Source: [NHIA Act 2022, Section 13](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/NHIA-Act-2022-Gazetted-Copy.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**4. Basic Health Care Provision Fund**

The BHCPF supports access to a basic minimum package of healthcare services, particularly for poor and vulnerable populations.

It supports:

- Preventive healthcare.
- Medical consultations and treatment.
- Essential medicines and vaccines.
- Primary healthcare facilities.
- Emergency medical treatment.

The NHIA works with state health insurance agencies to facilitate access to these services.

Source: [NHIA, Basic Health Care Provision Fund](https://www.nhia.gov.ng/basic-health-care-provision-fund/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**5. How individuals access coverage**

Generally, individuals should contact their State Social Health Insurance Agency, complete the enrolment process, obtain their insurance identification and access healthcare through accredited facilities.

## **H5-GL**

**How does health insurance work in the United States under the Affordable Care Act?**

The Affordable Care Act (ACA), signed into law on 23 March 2010, is a major US healthcare reform law designed to make health insurance more accessible and affordable.

It introduced consumer protections, financial assistance for eligible individuals and families, and regulations governing health insurance coverage.

Source: [HealthCare.gov, Affordable Care Act](https://www.healthcare.gov/where-can-i-read-the-affordable-care-act/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**1. How people obtain health insurance**

Americans can obtain health insurance through several channels:

- **Employer-sponsored insurance:** Employers provide health insurance benefits to eligible employees.
- **Health Insurance Marketplace:** Individuals and families can purchase private insurance plans through the federal or state marketplaces.
- **Medicaid:** A joint federal and state programme providing free or low-cost coverage to eligible low-income individuals and families.
- **Medicare:** A federal health insurance programme primarily serving people aged 65 and older and certain younger people with disabilities.
- **CHIP:** Provides health coverage for eligible children and pregnant women in families that earn too much to qualify for Medicaid.

Source: [HealthCare.gov, Getting Marketplace Health Insurance](https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/getting-marketplace-health-insurance/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**2. Health Insurance Marketplace**

The ACA established health insurance marketplaces where individuals and families can compare and purchase insurance plans.

The marketplaces offer four main categories of plans:

- **Bronze:** Generally lower monthly premiums but higher out-of-pocket costs.
- **Silver:** Moderate premiums and out-of-pocket costs.
- **Gold:** Higher premiums but lower out-of-pocket costs.
- **Platinum:** Generally the highest premiums and lowest out-of-pocket costs.

Eligible consumers may receive premium tax credits and other financial assistance based on household income and other eligibility requirements.

Source: [HealthCare.gov, Marketplace Plans and Prices](https://www.healthcare.gov/apply-and-enroll/health-insurance-plans-estimator-overview/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**3. Essential health benefits**

The ACA requires non-grandfathered individual and small-group health insurance plans to cover ten essential health benefit categories:

1. Ambulatory patient services.
2. Emergency services.
3. Hospitalization.
4. Maternity and newborn care.
5. Mental health and substance-use disorder services.
6. Prescription drugs.
7. Rehabilitative and habilitative services.
8. Laboratory services.
9. Preventive and wellness services.
10. Pediatric services, including oral and vision care.

Source: [Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS), Essential Health Benefits](https://www.cms.gov/marketplace/resources/data/essential-health-benefits?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**4. Protection for people with pre-existing conditions**

One of the ACA's major protections is that insurance companies cannot refuse coverage, charge higher premiums or deny essential health benefits because someone has a pre-existing medical condition.

The law also provides certain preventive services without additional cost-sharing when delivered under applicable plan rules.

Sources:

- [HealthCare.gov, Pre-existing Conditions](https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage/pre-existing-conditions/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

**5. Financial assistance and affordability**

Eligible individuals and families can receive premium tax credits to reduce monthly insurance payments.

The amount of assistance depends on factors such as:

- Expected annual household income.
- Household size.
- Location.
- Eligibility for other qualifying health coverage.

The Marketplace also determines whether applicants qualify for Medicaid or CHIP.

Source: [HealthCare.gov, Income and Household Information](https://www.healthcare.gov/income-and-household-information/income/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

Individuals generally apply during the annual Open Enrollment Period. Certain qualifying life events, such as marriage, childbirth, relocation or losing existing health coverage, may allow enrolment outside that period through a Special Enrollment Period.

After selecting a plan and paying the required premium, the insured person receives coverage and can access covered services through the plan's healthcare provider network.
